# Designer advice (good practice — not rules)

The MagickHome rule book is the only set of rules. This advice helps you design like an experienced
kitchen designer where the rule book leaves the choice to you. It never overrides a rule or the
customer's stated needs. If the two seem to disagree, the rule book and the customer win.

## Reading the room
- Note every door: its wall, its position, and the corner it is near. Cabinets must not block a door or
  its swing. Keep the path from the door to the work area clear.
- Note every window: a wall run breaks at a window (R37), so wall cabinets go beside it, never across it.
- Long, uninterrupted walls carry the most storage and the longest counter. Short walls between a door
  and a corner usually suit a tall unit or nothing.
- Check the ceiling. When lofts fit, they add storage for big vessels and seasonal items.

## Choosing the layout
- Use the walls that give the household enough counter and storage without blocking doors or the
  window light. A small room often works best as a straight or L kitchen. A larger room can take a U.
- In a U, keep a comfortable aisle between the facing runs.
- The sink can sit anywhere the rules allow: on a long run, beside a window, or at a corner with a
  corner sink unit when the cabinet list offers one. Choose the place that gives the best working counter on both sides of it.

## Working zones
- Give the hob counter on both sides (the landings in the rules), keep it clear of a door, and keep a
  working counter between the sink and the hob so two people can work at once.
- Put the spice pull-out right beside the hob and the LPG cylinder unit next to it when gas is LPG.
- Keep tall units at the end of a run, not in the middle of the counter, not in front of a window and
  not beside the hob, where they would block the work.

## Storage
- Store things where they are used: drawers for pans and daily vessels near the hob, baskets or
  trolleys near the sink.
- Heavy daily cooking and big families need more drawers. Light cooking can use shutters.
- The budget tier decides drawers vs shutters and the finishes allowed (rule book). Follow it.

## Look and finish
- Use a lighter wall run when the base run is dark, so the room feels open.
- Follow the colour words in the customer's brief within the finishes the budget allows.
- Lift-up wall doors suit a clean, minimal look. Hinged shutters are the standard.

## Explaining your choices
- Give the real reason for each decision: the rule that set it (cite its R number), what the customer
  asked for, or the room fact that decided it (wall length, door, window, ceiling height).
- Never give a reason the room or the customer does not support.
