## playbook (29)

- **Why is the sink placed under the window? / Why should the sink go under the window?**  
  In MagickHome kitchens the sink is centred under the window: you get daylight and fresh air while washing, and a view. That makes the window wall the wet zone; a wall water purifier, if needed, hangs on the same wall beside the window.
- **Why can't the hob go on a wall with a window? / Can I put the hob under the window?**  
  The hob and chimney go on a solid wall with no window: wind from a window can blow the flame out, and the chimney needs a solid wall to mount on and vent from.
- **Can I put the hob right next to the sink? / Why not keep the hob beside the sink?**  
  Better not. In L and U kitchens MagickHome puts the sink and the hob on different walls. If they must share a wall, keep at least 600-900 mm of counter between them - that is your preparation space, and it keeps water away from the flame.
- **How far should the hob be from a door? / Can the hob be near the door?**  
  Keep the hob at least 600 mm from a door, out of the corners, with at least 300 mm of counter on both sides. A door next to the hob is a safety risk and a draught on the flame.
- **Where do tall units go? / Where should the tall pantry be placed?**  
  Tall units (pantry, built-in oven or microwave) go at the END of a run, ideally the end nearest the entrance. Never in front of a window and never right beside the hob.
- **Can I put a tall unit next to the hob? / Why not keep the pantry beside the hob?**  
  No - a tall unit right beside the hob blocks the counter you need while cooking and gets heat and grease on its side. Put it at the end of a run, ideally near the entrance.
- **What is the work triangle? / Explain the kitchen work triangle.**  
  The work triangle links the sink, the hob and the tall storage. Each leg should be about 1200-2700 mm, and no leg should cross the door path - short enough to save steps, long enough for counter space.
- **When should I choose an L-shaped kitchen? / When is an L-shape best?**  
  L-shape suits most rooms: the window wall plus one adjacent solid wall. Put the hob on the adjacent wall that is farther from the door.
- **When should I choose a U-shaped kitchen? / When is a U-shape possible?**  
  Choose U (the window wall plus both adjacent walls) only when the aisle between the two side runs is at least 1200 mm and both side walls have a useful free run. It gives the most storage and counter.
- **When is a single-wall kitchen used? / When should the kitchen be on one wall?**  
  A single-wall kitchen is for narrow rooms, or when the other walls are blocked by doors.
- **How much free wall does a cabinet run need? / Can a short wall carry cabinets?**  
  A wall with less than 1200 mm of free run cannot carry cabinets.
- **What sink size should I choose? / Which sink unit size is right?**  
  900 mm is the standard sink unit. Choose 1050 mm when the window wall has 3000+ mm free, 1200 mm (large or double bowl) only with 3600+ mm free, and 800 mm for small kitchens with under 2400 mm free. The rule book can also set the sink width by kitchen size.
- **What hob unit size should I choose? / 900 or 750 hob unit?**  
  900 mm normally, 750 mm when the hob wall has under 2400 mm free (the rule book sets the exact width by kitchen type and size band).
- **Should I choose a drawer or shutter hob unit? / Why a drawer hob unit?**  
  Prefer a drawer hob unit (3 or 2 drawers): heavy pans and pressure cookers lie flat and are easy to lift. A shutter hob unit wastes space.
- **Do I need a spice pull-out? / What size spice pull-out?**  
  Yes - one spice pull-out right beside the hob for masalas and oils. 300 mm is standard; 150 mm only in very tight runs.
- **Do I need an LPG cylinder unit? / Is the gas cylinder unit included?**  
  LPG is assumed unless you tell us you have piped gas (PNG) or induction (rule R11), so the cylinder unit is included. It sits on the hob's left, with the spice pull-out on its right (seen from the room).
- **I have piped gas. Do I need the cylinder unit? / We use induction - is the LPG unit needed?**  
  No. With piped gas (PNG) or induction the cylinder unit is dropped (rule R11); that space becomes normal storage.
- **Should I add lofts? / When are lofts possible?**  
  Most Indian kitchens have lofts above the wall cabinets for big vessels, festival items and bulk stock. They need a ceiling of at least 2658 mm. Without lofts the wall and tall units are made taller automatically, so storage still reaches the ceiling.
- **Hinged shutters or lift-up doors for wall cabinets? / Should I choose lift-up wall doors?**  
  Hinged shutters are the standard and cost least. Lift-up (flap-up) doors suit a modern look, or narrow kitchens where open doors would hit heads.
- **Should I add an open shelf? / Is an open shelf unit a good idea?**  
  At most one, at a run end away from the hob, for daily jars, tea and coffee - and only if you like the open look. Open shelves gather grease and dust.
- **Where should the crockery unit go? / Do I need a crockery unit?**  
  A crockery unit (glass tall unit) suits families who host guests or want a showcase. It goes at the run end nearest the dining area - never beside the hob or the sink.
- **Should I add a water purifier unit? / Where does the Aquaguard go?**  
  Most Indian homes use a wall water purifier. Its cabinet hangs on the sink wall beside the window. Skip it if you use an under-sink RO.
- **Drawers or shutters for base cabinets? / Which base cabinet style should I choose?**  
  Drawers for heavy daily cooking or 5+ people (pans and vessels stay reachable); mixed (a cutlery drawer on top, a shelf below) for most homes; shutters for light cooking or a tight budget.
- **Where does the vegetable wicker basket go? / Where should the grain trolley be?**  
  The vegetable wicker basket sits near the sink (wash, then cut). The grain trolley belongs in the storage zone, at a run end away from the hob heat.
- **What goes in the corners? / Can I put a normal cabinet in the corner?**  
  Corners take only corner cabinets (blind corner or L-corner units); a normal cabinet never goes in a corner. The designer adds them automatically.
- **How much counter is needed beside the hob and sink? / What is the minimum counter at the end of a run?**  
  Keep at least 450 mm of counter between the hob or the sink and the end of its run, and at least 300 mm on both sides of the hob.
- **What are the kitchen size bands? / How is a kitchen classed as small, medium or large?**  
  By floor area: Small 60-80 sqft, Medium 81-120 sqft, Large 121-180 sqft. The band decides module widths in the rule book.
- **How many hobs and sinks can a kitchen have?**  
  One hob, one sink and one spice pull-out per kitchen.
- **Which wall gets the hob if I choose primary and secondary walls?**  
  Your primary wall carries the hob and your secondary wall carries the sink.

## cabinets (18)

- **When should I use a Base Unit? / What is a Base Unit for?**  
  General base storage under the counter. Choose it when: Drawer versions next to the hob for utensils and pans; shutter versions for bulk storage, cleaning items and bins. Avoid: Many narrow single-shutter units in one run.
- **When should I use a Base Sink Unit? / What is a Base Sink Unit for?**  
  Carcass under the sink bowl with room for pipes, waste bin and cleaning items. Choose it when: Always one per kitchen, centred under the window. Avoid: Placing it on a wall without the window when a usable window wall exists.
- **When should I use a Base Hob Unit? / What is a Base Hob Unit for?**  
  Unit under the built-in hob; the chimney hangs above it. Choose it when: Always one per kitchen, on a solid wall. Drawer versions are the designer default. Avoid: Shutter versions (pans are hard to reach); placing it under a window or beside a door.
- **When should I use a Base Spice Pull Out? / What is a Base Spice Pull Out for?**  
  Tall narrow pull-out for masalas, oils and sauces. Choose it when: Always right beside the hob. Avoid: Placing it away from the hob.
- **When should I use a Base Cylinder Unit? / What is a Base Cylinder Unit for?**  
  Houses the LPG cylinder; 800 mm high, floor-standing for easy rolling. Choose it when: Kitchens on LPG with room beside the hob (hob wall 2700+ mm free). Avoid: Kitchens with piped gas; tight hob walls.
- **When should I use a Base Grain Trolley? / What is a Base Grain Trolley for?**  
  Pull-out bins for atta, rice and dal. Choose it when: Storage zone, beside a tall unit or at the end of a run. Avoid: Beside the sink (moisture).
- **When should I use a Base Wicker Basket Unit? / What is a Base Wicker Basket Unit for?**  
  Ventilated wicker baskets for onions, potatoes and vegetables. Choose it when: Near the sink (wash, then cut) or in the storage zone. Avoid: Beside the hob (heat).
- **When should I use a Wall Unit? / What is a Wall Unit for?**  
  Wall cabinets above the counter for daily dishes, glasses and dry stock. Choose it when: Above every base run except the window and the chimney. Avoid: Many narrow units; wall units over the window or chimney.

  Wall cabinet that houses the water purifier. Choose it when: When the household uses a wall-mounted purifier (most Indian homes): on the sink wall right beside the window (water inlet and power near the sink). Not needed with an under-sink RO. Avoid: Far from the sink; above the hob.
- **When should I use a Wall Glass Unit? / What is a Wall Glass Unit for?**  
  Glass-front wall cabinet to display crockery. Choose it when: One unit to finish a run or frame the window; lightens a long row of solid doors. Avoid: More than one or two; above the hob (grease).

  Shaker-style glass display wall cabinet. Choose it when: Shaker kitchens: one unit to finish a run. Avoid: Above the hob.

  Open shelf wall unit for daily-use jars, tea, coffee and cookbooks. Choose it when: One unit at a run end to break a long row of doors, or when daily items need quick reach. Avoid: Above or beside the hob (grease and dust settle on open shelves); more than one per kitchen.
